# Multimedia & On-Device AI — week of 5 October to 11 October 2026

*Google opened its SynthID Detector to the public and published a joint Arm/Google optimization of on-device GPU inference; otherwise a quiet week among the principals, with the most eye-catching claim — a reported Samsung exit from automotive application processors — unconfirmed by Samsung.*

## Top items

### Google opens its SynthID AI-content detector to the public
**Google** · content provenance · 7 October 2026 · Shipped

Google DeepMind made SynthID Detector — a tool for checking whether an image, video or audio file carries a SynthID watermark — available to everyone globally in English at synthid.com, having previously limited it to a waitlist of journalists and researchers. Detection now also covers watermarks from partner models rather than only Google's own: OpenAI, NVIDIA and Kakao are covered now, with Apple listed as "coming soon." Google says its built-in verification features in Search, the Gemini app and Chrome handle over 1 million watermark-check requests a day, and that more than 180 billion images and videos and roughly 240,000 years of audio have been watermarked with SynthID since the system launched in 2023.

**Why it matters on this beat.** SynthID is not C2PA, but it performs a version of the same function content-provenance standards aim for — letting a viewer check after the fact whether media was AI-made — and widening free public access to it, across multiple vendors' models rather than only Google's, raises the baseline for what any content-verification tool now needs to cover.

[Source](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/models-and-research/google-deepmind/synth-id-ai-content/) · 7 October 2026

### Verkada wires Claude, ChatGPT and Gemini into its security-camera platform, and adds a thermal camera
**Verkada** · security/surveillance camera, on-device AI · 8 October 2026 · Announced (mixed; see below)

Verkada's October 2026 product update, effective 8 October, introduces Catalyst — branded "Verkada MCP" — which connects its Command platform to Claude, ChatGPT and Gemini for AI-driven workflows and reports; Verkada says it is "initially available as a private beta." The same release adds a new AI search model for multi-condition safety queries, AI voice receivers for intercom call flows, and the CTB63, a dual-spectrum thermal bullet camera (wide-angle and telephoto variants) that layers people- and vehicle-analytics on a combined visible-and-thermal feed. Verkada states no price and no ship date for the CTB63 beyond the October release window. Six camera models aimed at government buyers are also listed as meeting the FIPS 140 encryption standard and TAA/NDAA compliance.

**Why it matters on this beat.** A physical-security camera vendor wiring its command platform directly into three separate general-purpose AI assistants — rather than building or branding a single in-house model — is a different integration choice than the camera-silicon vendors on this beat have made, and it arrives alongside hardware that pushes analytics past RGB alone by fusing in a thermal feed.

[Source](https://www.verkada.com/updates/october-2026-product-updates/) · 8 October 2026

## Also noted

- **Arm** — Published a joint engineering blog with Google AI Edge on Mali GPU optimizations for ML Drift, Google's cross-platform GPU compute engine for on-device inference (the layer behind the LiteRT ML Drift accelerator), covering compute-kernel design, texture-cache and memory-bandwidth efficiency, and device-aware workgroup tuning across Arm GPU generations. Google AI Edge is open-sourcing ML Drift under Apache 2.0. No benchmark figures were given. [source](https://developer.arm.com/community/arm-community-blogs/b/ai-blog/posts/how-arm-and-google-are-improving-ml-drift-performance-on-arm-gpus) (8 October 2026)
- **Samsung System LSI** — Rumoured: Korean outlet Munhwa Ilbo reported Samsung's System LSI division has decided to wind down development of future Exynos Auto application processors, finishing existing commitments to Hyundai and BMW before exiting the business; the report, relayed by SamMobile and Gadget Hacks, cites development cost of over 100 billion won (roughly $75 million) per chip generation against a per-unit chip price of $20–40 as the stated rationale. Samsung has not confirmed the report, and denied a similar one in December 2025. [source](https://samsung.gadgethacks.com/news/samsung-exiting-exynos-auto-chip-business-whats-confirmed/) (reported on or before 8 October 2026)
