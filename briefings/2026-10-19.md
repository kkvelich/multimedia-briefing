# Multimedia & On-Device AI — week of 12 October to 18 October 2026

*Apple's "Welcome Home" event put specific, named architecture terms on where camera and microphone processing happens in two new products; DJI shipped its first drone dock with automated battery swapping. Otherwise a quiet week among the nine principals.*

## Top items

### Apple names its on-device/cloud split for camera and microphone data in two new home devices
**Apple** · on-device AI for media, camera/imaging silicon · 13 October 2026 · Announced (pre-order; general availability 13 November 2026)

At an event Apple branded "Welcome Home," the company unveiled HomeView, a $299 smart display with a built-in camera, FaceTime and Center Stage framing, and a fourth-generation Apple TV 4K, also $299, built on the A19 chip with a new dedicated AV1 decoder. Both run a rebuilt "Siri AI," which Apple states runs "on-device processing or Private Cloud Compute" depending on the request, and both process camera and microphone data in what Apple calls "Secure Exclaves" on the device's chip — on HomeView, the A18 — which the company says "cannot be accessed by the [device] software, apps, the user, or Apple." With a supported iCloud+ plan, Apple Intelligence also generates camera-activity descriptions and summarizes Home-camera notifications on both devices. Pre-orders opened 13 October 2026; general availability begins 13 November 2026 in the US, Australia, Canada, India, New Zealand, Singapore, South Africa and the UK — not the EU, where Apple says Siri AI is unavailable in iOS.

**Why it matters on this beat.** Vendors on this beat rarely state in public where media processing actually runs; Apple naming a specific split — on-device versus a named cloud tier, with camera/microphone data walled off in hardware even from its own software — gives a concrete reference point for the "edge versus cloud" question this briefing otherwise has to infer from indirect evidence like pricing or connectivity requirements.

### DJI ships its first automated battery-swapping drone dock
**DJI** · drones, robotics vision · 14 October 2026 · Announced (enterprise/dealer channel; no price or ship date given)

DJI Enterprise announced Dock 4, which DJI says is its first dock to swap a drone's battery automatically rather than only recharging it in place; the company states a swap completes in 25 seconds under controlled test conditions, measured from dock-cover close to swap completion, with flight data transferred automatically during the exchange. It launched alongside the Matrice 5TD, which carries telephoto, infrared, laser-ranging and full-color night-vision imaging plus three-directional mmWave radar, rotating LiDAR and six-directional vision sensing (DJI notes mmWave radar availability varies by region). DJI's FlightHub 2 software adds a "Copilot" that converts plain-language instructions into flight commands and can apply AI post-processing to turn flight footage into reports; the company states that starting a dispatch workflow from a third-party alert still requires an operator to confirm it. DJI gave no price and said the dock is available through its enterprise dealer network, directing interested buyers to request a local demonstration.

**Why it matters on this beat.** Automated battery swapping removes the main limit on continuous drone-based monitoring — recharge downtime — extending the "always-on camera" case that fixed security cameras have had for years into aerial coverage; DJI's choice to still require human confirmation before an automated alert triggers a flight is itself a data point on how much autonomy a vendor is willing to claim for this kind of system today.

[Source](https://enterprise-insights.dji.com/blog/dji-dock-4-battery-swapping-dock) · 14 October 2026
