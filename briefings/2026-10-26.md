## Top items

### Google and Synaptics ship the first commercial chips built on the open Coral NPU standard
**Google Research / Synaptics** · on-device AI for media · 20 October 2026 · Announced / Shipped (Coralboard SL available now; Astra SR200 series taped out)

At a Synaptics "Tech Day" event in San Jose, Google Research and Synaptics announced the Astra SR200 series, a family of three pin-compatible "xMCU" processors — SR210 (200 GOPS), SR230 (300 GOPS, adds vision and display), and SR250 (400 GOPS, dual vision/display) — built around Synaptics' Torq AI engine paired with Google's Coral NPU, running on Arm Cortex-M85/M52 cores. Google separately announced that the full 32-bit Coral NPU architecture and its open-source MLIR/IREE-based Coral Compiler v1.0 are complete and published, alongside a Matrix Execution Unit configurable up to 1 TOPS that Google says targets roughly 10 mW average inference power. The companies also launched the Coralboard SR development kit (built on the SR250) and said the existing Coralboard SL — built on the Synaptics Astra SL2619 with a 1 TOPS Torq/Coral NPU — is now generally available worldwide through DigiKey, its exclusive distributor. Arm EVP Chris Bergey and Google Research VP Yossi Matias were quoted alongside Synaptics executives; Sonos and Verisure were named as companies evaluating the platform for audio and home-security products respectively.

**Why it matters on this beat.** This is the Coral NPU open standard's first appearance in shipping, purchasable silicon rather than a reference design — a test of whether an open NPU architecture, backed by Google and Arm's compute cores, can compete with the proprietary NPU blocks that Qualcomm, MediaTek and Apple each build in-house for the always-on, low-power inference tier that cameras, wearables and smart-home sensors depend on.

[Source](https://www.synaptics.com/company/news/synaptics-astra-sr200-series-multimodal-edge-ai-processors) · 20 October 2026

### Sony Semiconductor Solutions shrinks a digital image sensor for single-use endoscopes
**Sony Semiconductor Solutions** · camera and imaging silicon · 22 October 2026 · Announced (samples planned November 2026)

Sony announced the IMX742, a 1/17.7-type back-illuminated CMOS sensor with a 1.00 mm × 1.00 mm chip size and roughly 0.51-megapixel (720×720) resolution, aimed at thin, disposable endoscopes such as bronchoscopes and ureteroscopes. Sony states it is the industry's first sensor of its kind to carry both digital video and bidirectional control signals over the same cable across a 5-meter run, verified in Sony's own test setup using AWG44 cable, at 30 or 60 fps. The company explicitly states the part "is not a medical device and has not been evaluated or approved for medical applications" — it is a component sensor, not a cleared diagnostic product. Sample shipments are planned for November 2026.

**Why it matters on this beat.** It is a narrow application, but it is a direct data point on how far sensor-and-interface miniaturization has moved: a fully digital video link, with control signaling folded onto the same conductor, now fits inside a 1mm-square die meant to be thrown away after one use.

[Source](https://www.sony-semicon.com/en/news/2026/2026102201.html) · 22 October 2026
