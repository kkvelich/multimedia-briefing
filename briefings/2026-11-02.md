# Multimedia & On-Device AI — week of 26 October to 1 November 2026

*Apple refreshed its Neural Engine across three product lines and paired the hardware with an on-device AI update to its own creative software; separately, Google and XREAL shipped the first wired glasses built on Android XR, debuting Qualcomm's Snapdragon Reality Elite platform in a product for the first time.*

## Top items

### Apple refreshes iPad mini, iMac and MacBook Pro with new Neural Engine silicon
**Apple** · on-device AI for media · 26–28 October 2026 · Announced (rolling out; some models ship 6 November)

Over three consecutive days, Apple introduced new chips across three product lines, each pitched on faster on-device AI and imaging. The **iPad mini** (26 October) gets the **A20 Pro**, built on a 2nm process, with a dual 16-core Neural Engine Apple says doubles the prior generation's processing power and delivers "up to 4.5x" overall AI performance versus the previous iPad mini; Apple ties this to on-device Siri AI voice generation and dictation, alongside a 12MP rear camera with Smart HDR 4 and an Adaptive True Tone flash. The 14-inch **MacBook Pro with M6** and the **iMac with M6** (both 27 October) share a dual 16-core Neural Engine that Apple says runs on-device AI workloads "up to 25 percent faster" than M5 and LLM prompt processing up to 1.3x faster than M5 (iMac: up to 5x faster on-device AI than M4, and up to 170GB/s memory bandwidth for running LLMs locally). The **14-inch and 16-inch MacBook Pro with M5 Pro and M5 Max** (28 October) add a Neural Accelerator inside each GPU core, which Apple says lets the M5 Max run language models with "hundreds of billions of parameters" entirely on-device. All figures are Apple's own benchmarks, comparing against earlier Apple chips rather than any competitor's part.

**Why it matters on this beat.** Apple is now shipping its fourth consecutive generation to lead with on-device Neural Engine gains rather than CPU or GPU headline numbers, and extending the pitch — running large models locally, not just accelerating photo/video filters — down into its cheapest tablet (iPad mini) as well as its workstation silicon. None of Qualcomm, MediaTek, Samsung System LSI, Ambarella, NVIDIA, Arm or Sony Semiconductor Solutions made a comparable statement this week; Google's own move this week, covered separately below, was in a different product category.

### Apple moves its own creative software onto on-device and model-based AI
**Apple** · media software · 27 October 2026 · Announced (arrives on the App Store November 2026; Freeform feature in December)

Apple detailed AI updates across its creative apps. Logic Pro gets a production assistant (U.S. English, beta, requires an Apple Intelligence-capable Mac) built on "a combination of Apple-built and third-party models," plus an "on-device audio analysis tool" that classifies and tags sound libraries, and a Stem Splitter that isolates or rebalances drum parts without the original multitrack. Keynote, Pages and Numbers gain a context-aware assistant built on "Apple models and OpenAI," plus Model Context Protocol (MCP) support that Apple says "enables external AI agents and tools to read content, perform edits, and execute automated actions." Pixelmator Pro adds a Generate Fill tool and a Super Resolution upscaler; Final Cut Pro and Motion can generate and edit images from text descriptions and round-trip them to Pixelmator Pro. The release does not state where the Keynote/Pages/Numbers or Pixelmator Pro processing runs.

**Why it matters on this beat.** This is a case where Apple is explicit, feature by feature, about which AI runs on-device (Logic Pro's audio analysis) and which is model-based without a stated location (the document assistants, Generate Fill) — a useful contrast to track as other media-software vendors describe their own AI features.

[Source](https://www.apple.com/newsroom/2026/10/apple-creator-studio-adds-powerful-new-ai-features-and-even-more-creative-tools/) · 27 October 2026

### Google and XREAL ship the first wired Android XR glasses, debuting Qualcomm's Snapdragon Reality Elite platform
**Google / XREAL / Qualcomm** · form factors (XR) · 28 October 2026 · Shipped (orders open; shipments mid-November)

Google and XREAL launched XREAL AURA, which Google's announcement — written by Shahram Izadi, VP & GM of XR — calls "the first wired XR glasses powered by Android XR." The glasses themselves weigh under 95 grams and carry a 70-degree field of view with transparent, dimmable lenses; processing and battery live in a tethered "compute puck" that Google says is the first product to carry Qualcomm's Snapdragon Reality Elite platform (a chip Qualcomm announced separately in June 2026). Gemini provides on-glasses scene and screen understanding; neither company's post states where that processing runs, and no camera or sensor specifications were disclosed. Orders opened 28 October in the US, Canada and Korea (Japan follows 2 November), with shipments via XREAL and Best Buy beginning mid-November at a launch price of $1,099 "through the end of the year." Qualcomm issued no newsroom release of its own marking the debut; the only public statement on it is Google's.

**Why it matters on this beat.** It is the first time Qualcomm's XR-specific compute platform has shipped inside a real product rather than appearing only in Qualcomm's own announcement of the chip, and it keeps processing split between a worn display and a tethered compute unit rather than putting the SoC in the glasses themselves — a form-factor choice distinct from the untethered designs Meta and others have pursued.

[Source](https://blog.google/products-and-platforms/platforms/android/xreal-aura/) · 28 October 2026
