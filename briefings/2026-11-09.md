# Multimedia & On-Device AI — week of 2 November to 8 November 2026

*Google, NVIDIA and Arm each published quantified claims this week about running generative AI and neural rendering closer to — or entirely off — local hardware, while four of the nine principals (Qualcomm, Ambarella, Samsung System LSI, Sony Semiconductor Solutions) had nothing on this beat in their newsrooms.*

## Top items

### DJI ships the Matrice 5T, an enterprise drone that fuses thermal, visible, radar and lidar sensing on-board
**DJI** · form factor (drone) · 5 November 2026 · shipped

DJI's enterprise division released the Matrice 5T, available immediately through authorized dealers. It carries a wide-angle camera (1/0.98" sensor, f/1.8, ISO up to 1,600,000, AI low-light enhancement), a 50MP telephoto with 3x optical/11x optical-plus-hybrid-176x zoom, 1K native thermal imaging (up to 2560×2048 in UHR mode), lidar and mmWave radar. DJI says on-board AI models fuse the thermal and visible feeds to detect more than 20 target types and support text- or image-prompt search through DJI Pilot 2/FlightHub2, plus support for running third-party models on-device. The aircraft is rated IP55 with a 59-minute flight time and a 40km O4 Enterprise link.

**Why it matters on this beat.** It is a concrete, shipping example of multi-sensor AI fusion running on the aircraft itself rather than a ground station or cloud — the edge end of the edge/cloud split this beat tracks, in a commercial rather than consumer form factor.

[Source](https://enterprise.dji.com/news/detail/matrice-5t-release) · 5 November 2026

### Apple's redesigned Health app runs on-device computer vision on iPhone camera input for movement evaluations
**Apple** · on-device AI for media · 3 November 2026 · announced

Alongside expanding Health app language support to ten more locales, Apple detailed "at-home movement evaluations" that use the iPhone camera together with Apple Watch to assess flexibility, strength, balance and VO2 max, feeding new Insights, For You and Longevity tabs (including a "Health Age" metric). Apple states the evaluations use "on-device vision-based AI models" and that the feature "runs entirely on device; no video is ever recorded, stored, or shared." The capability is live in the iOS 27.3 developer beta; Apple says public rollout comes "later this year."

**Why it matters on this beat.** It is a named, mainstream instance of a company stating explicitly that camera-based inference stays on the device rather than going to the cloud — the kind of explicit architecture claim this beat usually has to infer from pricing or connectivity requirements instead.

[Source](https://www.apple.com/newsroom/2026/11/redesigned-health-app-expands-language-support/) · 3 November 2026

### Google ships Gemini Nano 4 and stabilizes the Android XR developer platform at Android Developer Summit
**Google** · on-device AI / form factor (XR) · 5–6 November 2026 · shipped (Gemini Nano 4, Jetpack XR core) / announced (ML Kit Agentic API, alpha)

At Android Developer Summit, Google detailed Gemini Nano 4 — built on Gemma 4, using per-layer embeddings, quantization-aware training and multi-token prediction — which it says already powers on-device GenAI features in production, with up to 3x faster inference and up to 4x lower memory use than the previous Gemini Nano on Google's own internal benchmarks (not independently verified). ML Kit GenAI gained a new on-device Agentic API that executes function calls and manages its own orchestration loop, plus an alpha Embeddings API, both running without a server call. Separately, Jetpack SceneCore, ARCore for Jetpack XR and the XR Runtime moved from preview to stable, as did the Android XR Engine Hub for Unity, Godot and Unreal — the developer-tooling layer beneath Android XR devices such as the XREAL AURA that debuted the prior week.

**Why it matters on this beat.** Two separate claims point the same direction: a quantified efficiency gain for on-device models, and a graduation of the XR software stack from preview to production-grade — the precondition for an Android XR hardware wave beyond a single launch device.

[Source: Gemini Nano 4](https://developer.android.com/blog/posts/android-intelligence-system-in-app-agents-ads) · 5 November 2026 · [Source: Android XR SDK](https://developer.android.com/blog/posts/build-android-xr-devices-tools) · 6 November 2026

### NVIDIA moves neural rendering into the cloud subscription, not the local GPU, for DLSS 5's Hogwarts Legacy debut
**NVIDIA** · media software / edge-cloud split · 5 November 2026 · shipped

NVIDIA says DLSS 5 with "3D-Guided Neural Rendering" arrived in Hogwarts Legacy, adding AI-generated lighting and material detail while "retaining underlying geometry and textures," tuned by developer Avalanche Software through DLSS 5's developer controls. NVIDIA's own post names no local RTX hardware for this feature; it describes the update as available to GeForce NOW Ultimate subscribers streaming "GeForce RTX 5080-class performance" across PCs, Macs, handhelds, browsers, mobile devices and compatible TVs, with no local hardware upgrade or download needed. The page gives no FPS or resolution figures.

**Why it matters on this beat.** NVIDIA is routing a new neural-rendering feature through its own cloud-streaming tier rather than (or at least ahead of) local silicon — a vendor choosing the cloud side of the edge/cloud split for a feature that, on past DLSS generations, shipped to local GPUs first.

[Source](https://blogs.nvidia.com/blog/geforce-now-thursday-hogwarts-legacy-dlss-5/) · 5 November 2026

### Arm publishes a case study of a shipped mobile game running generative AI on CPU, not GPU or NPU
**Arm** · on-device AI · 4 November 2026 · shipped

Arm's developer blog documents how mobile-game studio Studio Atelico uses the Scalable Matrix Extension 2 (SME2) CPU feature in "Bobium Brawlers" to run a small language model (deck/card generation), an image model (creature artwork) and embedding-based content-safety classifiers entirely on the CPU, freeing the GPU for rendering. Arm cites specific figures from the deployment: a content-safety check in roughly 2ms against a 200ms target; an SME2 image-generation path cut from 46 seconds to 16 seconds; decoder throughput on SME2 rising from 125.6 to 290.9 tokens/second for SmolLM2 135M and from 41.7 to 65.1 tokens/second for Qwen3 0.6B; INT4 quantization for the language model and BF16 for the image model.

**Why it matters on this beat.** Most on-device AI claims on this beat are about NPUs or GPUs; this is a concrete, numbers-backed case for doing generative inference on the CPU instead, in a shipped consumer app rather than a benchmark.

[Source](https://developer.arm.com/community/arm-community-blogs/b/ai-blog/posts/bringing-bobium-brawlers-to-android-with-arm-sme2) · 4 November 2026

## Standards and regulation

**UK government commits to a new statutory framework for police use of facial recognition, leaves private-sector use non-statutory.** The UK government published its response to a consultation that closed in February 2026, committing to statutory rules — initially covering facial images, with a mechanism to extend to other biometric types later — governing law-enforcement and public-body use of facial recognition and biometrics, backed by a statutory Code of Practice and a single new independent oversight body with enforcement powers (consolidating the existing Biometrics and Surveillance Camera Commissioner and Forensic Science Regulator roles). Private-sector use, such as retail CCTV, is explicitly not covered by the new statutory framework; it remains governed by existing UK data-protection law, with a separate non-statutory Code of Practice for crime-prevention use still to come. This directly shapes legal exposure for any camera or facial-recognition deployment in the UK, on both sides of the public/private line, though the legislation itself has not yet been introduced. [Source](https://www.gov.uk/government/consultations/legal-framework-for-using-facial-recognition-in-law-enforcement/outcome/new-legal-framework-for-law-enforcement-use-of-biometrics-facial-recognition-and-similar-technologies-government-response-to-the-consultation-acces) (4 November 2026)
